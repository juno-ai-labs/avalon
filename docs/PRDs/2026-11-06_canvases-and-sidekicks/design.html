Rally, in two modes: Canvases and Sidekicks

Mockups for the pivot in the PRD. Every screen is drawn in the current visual language: the 76px section rail, the 340px list sidebar, the centered header pill, pastel marks from the project palette, the pill composer, and the light and dark tokens in config/design-tokens/. Frames are 1440×900, the desktop app at its default size.

A Canvas is a live artifact several people edit at once, with Rally as one more editor. A Sidekick is an AI agent several people talk to and share. Channels, Threads, Breakouts, Activity and the Pinned section go away.

1. Canvases: the section and its home page

The rail has two sections. The sidebar lists Canvases, with a Pinned group on top (the same drag-to-pin pattern the current lists use) and a green dot where people are in the Canvas right now. The home page reuses the Sidekicks library layout: title, black “New” pill, search, filter chips.

2. A Canvas, open

The Canvas fills the main pane. The header pill names it; on the right are the people in it now, Share, and a menu. Other people’s cursors and selections show live. Rally edits like a person: its pending change shimmers where it lands. The right panel has two tabs. Chat is the Canvas’s own conversation with Rally, open to everyone in the Canvas. Comments holds the pinned notes (today’s artifact annotations).

4. A Sidekick, open

One conversation per Sidekick, shared by everyone it is shared with. People talk to each other there; a message addressed to the Sidekick starts a run, and each run lands in the same transcript as a collapsed row carrying its reply. Managers also edit the Sidekick in this conversation through the wizard, as today. What a run makes is a Canvas card that opens in the Canvases section. The details column keeps today’s Sidekick page content (status, Pause, who it can act as, schedule) and adds triggers, tools and the Canvases it writes to.
