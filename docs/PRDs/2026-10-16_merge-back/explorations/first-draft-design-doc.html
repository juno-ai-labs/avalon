Merging work back up

How a Breakout hands its result to its Thread, and a Thread hands its result to its Channel, without copying the conversation.

SummaryWhat a merge isPrior artEdge casesMerge strategiesLength budgetsUX by levelArchitecturePhasingOpen questions

Summary

  • One primitive: the Outcome. A small, typed record a person (or Rally, when asked) posts from a source conversation into its parent. It has a one-line headline, a short optional body, links to artifacts, pointers to source messages, a status, and a link back. Every merge strategy produces this same object; only how its fields get filled differs.
  • Five ways to fill it, offered in one composer: pick a message, pick an artifact, let Rally draft a decision record, ask Rally to write a wrap-up inside the source, or write it yourself. Rally preselects the best guess, so the common case is one click.
  • Never a transcript copy. Text is capped per level. Anything longer becomes or stays an artifact, and the Outcome links to it. Artifacts merge as live links, not snapshots.
  • One hop at a time. Breakout to Thread, Thread to Channel. A Thread's Outcome can cite its Breakouts' Outcomes, so summaries compose upward from small inputs.
  • Re-merge updates in place. A second merge from the same source becomes revision 2 of the same Outcome, not a second post. The card shows when the source has moved on since.
  • Exposure is explicit. The composer says who will newly see this before you post. Merging is the one deliberate way private work becomes visible, and it can be removed from the parent but not unseen.

What a merge is, and what it is not

A merge moves the resolution up a level. The source conversation stays where it is, whole and reachable. The parent gains one small item that says what was decided or made, who made it, and where to look for the detail.

It is

A handoff written for the parent's readers. Short, specific, linked. The equivalent of a squash commit message, not the commit history.

It is not

Moving messages, forwarding a transcript, or reprinting a long answer. Not a notification about activity either: activity already has Activity.

The navigation PRD already promises this: "When a thread is done, its result goes back to the channel, where the next person finds it." Today nothing carries that result. The Channel feed shows each thread's AI description, which tracks what the thread is about, never what it concluded.

Prior art worth taking

Product What it does What we take
Linear Resolve a comment thread by choosing one reply as the resolution; the rest collapse. Picking a message is a pointer, not a copy.
Slack "Also send to #channel", at compose time or later from the message menu. Promotion is per item, opt-in, and can happen after the fact.
Mattermost spec The channel copy's Delete becomes "Remove from channel"; the thread reply stays. Un-merge is separate from deleting the source.

Figma branches Review and merge with a description; the branch is archived but reachable. Optional "Merge and archive" for the source.
Zulip Resolve a topic: ✔ prefix, a small bot notice, unresolve soon after deletes the notice. A state mark on the source, a tiny event in the parent, undo that leaves no trace.

Microsoft Loop One live component embedded in many places. Artifacts merge as live references with a "since merge" signal.

From research: dialogue summaries are unfaithful often enough to matter (over 35% in one human study; LLM judges catch this poorly), and the typical failure is stating an agreement that was only implied. Readers rarely click sources. Both point the same way: a person confirms every AI-drafted Outcome, claims cite source messages, and short is safer than long. Group-chat studies favour typed outcomes (decision, action, question) over free prose. Context collapse research is a reminder that a Breakout was written for a small audience, so the Outcome should be newly written for the wider one rather than quoted raw.

Edge cases

Choosing what to merge

Case What goes wrong Handling
Last message is Q&A Someone asked "how did you get 38%?" after the draft. The last message is the explanation, not the result. Never default to "last message". Rally ranks candidates (see smart default) and skips short replies that follow a long answer.
Small follow-up edits A long answer, then "change Q3 to Q4" and "drop the second table". No single message holds the final text. If an artifact exists, its latest version is the answer. If not, offer Consolidate: Rally rewrites the long message with the later edits applied, and the person checks the diff.
Several artifacts A draft, a revised draft, a data sheet, a scratch file. List every artifact the source generated, newest version first, with drafts superseded by a later version grayed out. Allow several; preselect the most recently edited deliverable.
Decision spread over many turns The answer was agreed across a dozen turns over three days. No message says it. Decision record: Rally drafts Decision, Why, Open questions, each line citing the turns it rests on.
No resolution Exploration that went nowhere, or work paused. Status Open or Update instead of Done. Or archive without merging; nothing forces a merge.
Rich content The picked message has a table, an image, code. The card shows the first block that fits the budget and a "Show more". Tables over the budget become a CSV or doc artifact on merge.
Mentions inside merged text The text mentions someone who is not in the parent. Mention chips render, but merging does not notify people outside the parent's audience. The composer flags it.

Size

Case Handling
Picked message is long The card shows the headline plus the first lines that fit; the full message is one click away in the source (if the reader can open it) or in the expanded card. Over the hard budget, the composer offers Save as doc and link it.
AI draft runs long The draft is generated against the level's budget, and the composer shows a live count against it. The budget is advisory up to the hard cap.

Audience and privacy

Case Handling
Private Breakout to its Thread The merged text and linked artifacts become visible to the Thread's whole audience. The composer names that audience ("Visible to 6 people in this thread" / "Visible to everyone in Acme") before Post. The Breakout itself stays private; source citations show as "in a private breakout" to people outside it.
Breakout member who is not in the Thread Breakout members can include people who cannot read the Thread. Only someone who can post in the parent may merge. Others see Ask to merge, which drafts the Outcome and @mentions a breakout member who can.
Private Thread into an Organization-wide Channel Today a private thread is a locked row that reveals nothing. Merging is the deliberate exception: the Outcome card is visible to Channel readers, while the thread chip stays "Request access". The composer says so plainly. Artifacts need their own share choice (see next row).
Artifacts in a wider audience For a Thread target, merging links the artifact into the Thread with the existing Attach to thread path, which grants the Thread's readers. A Channel has no conversation to link to, so a Channel merge needs a decision: either the artifact is shared to the Organization (or the Channel's members) through its own share setting, or an active Outcome becomes a new source of readers. The second is a new access source, and space-access-sources.md records why those are costly; the first is recommended. The composer lists each artifact with its new audience either way.
AI draft leaks candid remarks The summarizer reads only the source, writes for the parent's audience (instructed to drop asides, personal remarks and unverified claims), and the person confirms. Citations can only point inside the source.
Thread in several Channels Default target is the Channel it was started from; the composer lets you add the others. One Outcome, several placements, like threads today.
Guests Guests in the parent see the Outcome. A guest's Channel feed lists only threads they may open, so a Channel Outcome reaches a guest only if they can open its thread.
Undo "Remove from thread/channel" hides the card and its feed item and leaves the source untouched. Removal soon after posting deletes the event entirely, as Zulip does. Anyone who already read it has read it; the composer copy does not promise otherwise.

Lifecycle

Case Handling
More work after a merge The source keeps a merge waterline (the last turn included). The parent card shows "4 new messages in the breakout since merge" to people who can open it. Update merge in the source makes revision 2.
Re-merge Same Outcome, new revision. The card updates in place with "Updated 2h ago · v2" and a version list. The parent gets a small "updated" line, not a second card. Unread is bumped only when headline or status changed.
Artifact edited after merge Artifacts are live links, so readers see the latest. The card marks "Edited since merge" and keeps the merged version one click away.
Source archived The card stays; its back-link opens the archived source read-only. Merge and archive does both in one step for finished work.
Artifact deleted or access revoked The chip renders as unavailable; the headline and body still stand, because they were written into the Outcome.
Two people merge at once The second post becomes revision 2 and the composer warns "Maya merged this 1 min ago. Update hers?" before posting.
Several Breakouts, one Thread Each Breakout has its own Outcome under its origin chip. A later Thread merge can use those Outcomes as sources, and its card says "Includes 3 breakouts".
Skipping a level No Breakout to Channel. It would bypass the Thread's readers and make the hierarchy two things. A Thread merge built from Breakout Outcomes covers the need.

Agents and context

Case Handling
Does the Thread's agent see it? Yes. The Outcome lands in the Thread's transcript as a turn the agent reads ("Outcome merged from breakout Draft PRD: …"), so later questions in the Thread use it. It does not wake the agent, matching conversation_post.
Later Breakouts A new Breakout's frozen capture keeps only text and activation turns today. Outcomes must be added to that list, or a Breakout started later will not know about earlier results.
Rally merges on request "When the draft is approved, merge it back" is a person's instruction. Rally gets a merge_outcome tool scoped to its own parent; per product policy, it runs without a confirmation dialog. The card shows "Merged by Rally for Maya".
Rally suggests a merge When a Breakout goes quiet after a deliverable, the source shows a quiet "Ready to merge?" chip with the draft prefilled. No unprompted posting into the parent.
Sidekick runs Already post results with conversation_post. They can adopt the Outcome shape later so their results look the same; not needed first.

Feed, unread and notifications

Case Handling
Thread unread An Outcome is a message in the Thread, so it is unread for the Thread's people like any message.
Channel unread Today only a placement moves a Channel's dot. An Outcome is the Channel's own news in the same way, so it should stamp the waterline. Activity inside threads still does not.
Activity New Activity arm: "Maya merged an outcome into Q4 Pricing". Audience is the parent's readers; for a Breakout merge, the source's people are not notified twice.
Digests Outcomes are the most digest-worthy thing Rally produces. A digest can lead with them.
Ordering in the Channel feed The Outcome appears as a new item at the time of merge (newest, near the composer). The thread's own row gets a Done mark and its summary line switches to the Outcome headline.
Older clients and iOS Old desktops parse feed kinds and message types strictly. The Thread turn uses an existing message type with typed structured content and a plain fallback text; the Channel feed kind is opt-in by query flag, as originTurnId was.

Merge strategies

Merging varies along six independent dimensions. Each strategy below is a set of choices along them, not a separate feature.

Dimension Options Recommended default
Source of content Picked message · Artifact · Decision record (AI) · Wrap-up turn (AI, in source) · Written by hand Smart default picks one; all five in the composer
Shape in parent Pointer (one line) · Card (headline + body) · Embed (artifact preview) · Inline (full text) Card. Inline only for short text under budget
Freshness Snapshot · Live Text is a snapshot; artifacts are live with a "since merge" mark
Who starts it Person · Rally on request · Rally suggests · Automatic on archive Person or Rally on request. Never automatic
Review Post directly · Propose for a parent member to accept Post directly. Propose only when the merger cannot post in the parent
Repeat merges New post · Revise in place Revise in place

Five strategies

1 · Pointer

Pick one message. The card quotes its first lines and links to it. Cheapest, no AI. Best when one answer really is the result, which is common for short Breakouts.

2 · Deliverable

Pick one or more artifacts. Headline is the artifact title plus a one-line note. Best for drafts, PRDs, decks: the artifact already absorbed every edit, so "the last message is wrong" stops mattering.

3 · Decision record

Rally drafts Decision · Why · Open, each line citing the turns it came from. Best when the result is a choice made across many turns. Always human-confirmed.

4 · Wrap-up turn

"Wrap this up for the thread": Rally writes the handoff as a normal turn inside the source, where the person can say "shorter" or "add the risk" and iterate by chat. Then merge that turn as a Pointer. Reuses the conversation as the editor.

5 · Status update

For long-running Threads: an Outcome with status Update, revised weekly. The Channel shows the latest, with history. Same object, used as a living status line.

Written by hand

Always available. Fills the same fields. Also how anyone fixes an AI draft before posting.

Smart default

The composer opens with a preselected choice, so most merges are one click. Rules, in order:

  1. If the source generated an artifact that was edited in its last stretch of work, preselect Deliverable with the newest version.
  2. Else, if one agent message is long, structured, and followed only by short turns (questions, small edits), preselect Pointer to it; if those later turns asked for changes, offer Consolidate.
  3. Else, if the source has more than a few turns from more than one person, preselect Decision record.
  4. Else, preselect the last substantial agent message as a Pointer.

Rules 1, 2 and 4 are cheap heuristics over turn metadata. Rule 3 costs one model call and only runs when chosen. A later version can replace the rules with a single classifier call (the repo's JEV pattern) if the heuristics guess wrong often.

Length budgets

These are product numbers, for the team to set. The figures below are starting points to react to, not decisions.

Level Headline Body shown collapsed Body hard cap Over the cap
Breakout → Thread 1 line (~90 chars) ~4 lines ~250 words Save as doc, link it
Thread → Channel 1 line (~90 chars) 0 lines (headline + chips) ~120 words on expand Link the thread's artifact or doc

The Channel is tighter than the Thread because a Channel is browsed, while a Thread is read. A Channel headline must state the result itself ("Ship annual plan at $96/seat; monthly unchanged"), not name the work ("Pricing summary"). The draft prompt enforces this, and the composer greys the Post button while the headline is empty.

UX by level

In the source (Breakout or Thread)

  • Entry points. "Merge to thread" in the Breakout's title pill menu; "Merge this" on any agent message's action row (preselects that message); "Merge to thread" on an artifact's menu, next to the existing "Attach to thread"; and the "Ready to merge?" suggestion chip.
  • Composer. Opens as a sheet over the source pane, not a modal over the window, so the source stays readable beside it. Top: the five strategy tabs with the default selected. Middle: the live preview of the card exactly as the parent will show it. Bottom: audience line, status (Done / Update / Open), "Archive breakout after merging", Post.
  • After posting. The source shows a thin "Merged into Q4 Pricing · v1 · 2:14 PM" marker after the last included turn. Turns after it count as "since merge".

In the Thread

  • The Outcome lands where the transcript is at the moment of merge, as a turn: byline "Adam merged from ↳ Comparison table", then a card in the same sunken style as the Breakout's origin card: status mark, headline, body (collapsed), artifact chips, and "Open breakout · 23 messages". It is attributed to the person who merged, not to Rally. Placing it in time order is what the append-only transcript does anyway, and it is where people catching up are already reading.
  • The Breakout chip under the origin message gains a check and "Merged", and clicking the check scrolls to the Outcome. A reader scanning chips can tell finished side-work from open side-work. (The UI explorations also show the alternative: the card drawn under the origin message.)

In the Channel

  • A merged Thread's feed row changes from "what it is about" to "what it concluded": the Outcome headline replaces the description, with a Done mark, and one artifact chip beside the thread chip.
  • A new Outcome item also enters the feed at merge time, so people who check the bottom of the feed see new results without scanning older rows.
  • A newcomer to a busy Channel reads it as a list of results. Open work reads as descriptions; finished work reads as conclusions.

Technical architecture

Where it lives

The Channel has no transcript and runs no inference, and Threads and Breakouts are already hosted inside WorkspaceDO as parents. So WorkspaceDO owns the Outcome record for both levels: it already owns listing rules, the Channel feed, resource links, and Activity. The Thread transcript and the Channel feed are projections of that one record. This keeps one authority for who may see an Outcome, and avoids a second answer that can disagree with the first. 

-- WorkspaceDO SQLite (sketch)
CREATE TABLE outcomes (
  outcome_id        TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  source_kind       TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (source_kind IN ('breakout','thread')),
  source_id         TEXT NOT NULL,        -- breakout or thread id
  target_kind       TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (target_kind IN ('thread','channel')),
  target_id         TEXT NOT NULL,        -- thread id or space id
  state             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('active','removed')),
  current_revision  INTEGER NOT NULL,
  created_by_user_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  created_at TEXT NOT NULL, updated_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  UNIQUE (source_kind, source_id, target_kind, target_id)  -- re-merge = new revision
);
CREATE TABLE outcome_revisions (
  outcome_id TEXT NOT NULL, revision INTEGER NOT NULL,
  headline TEXT NOT NULL, body_md TEXT,
  status TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('done','update','open')),
  strategy TEXT NOT NULL,               -- pointer|deliverable|decision|wrapup|written
  source_through_turn_id TEXT NOT NULL, -- the merge waterline
  author_user_id TEXT NOT NULL, acting_agent INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  created_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (outcome_id, revision)
);
CREATE TABLE outcome_refs (               -- artifacts, source turns, child outcomes
  outcome_id TEXT NOT NULL, revision INTEGER NOT NULL,
  ref_kind TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (ref_kind IN ('resource','turn','outcome')),
  ref_id TEXT NOT NULL, resource_version INTEGER, position INTEGER NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (outcome_id, revision, ref_kind, ref_id)
);

Write path

1POST /conversations/:parentId/outcomes with source, target, fields, refs and an idempotency key. A Thread to Channel merge addresses the Thread's parent and names the Space. 2WorkspaceDO, in one transaction: check the author can read the source and post in the target; check every turn ref belongs to the source and every resource ref is linked to it; insert or revise the Outcome; for a Thread target, insert resource links (the same rows Attach to thread writes); for a Channel target, apply the artifact share chosen in the composer; stamp the Channel waterline for a Channel target; write the Activity event; queue the transcript projection. 3The projection leg appends one turn to the Thread's Main SubstreamAgent through the appendConversationPost path, keyed by outcomeId:revision so a retry is a no-op. It does not wake the agent. 4Sockets push the new turn to Thread readers and the feed item to Channel readers, the way placements and turns travel today.

The client draws the card optimistically from the request the moment Post is pressed, the same hand-off the new-thread composer uses, so merging feels instant.

Contracts and compatibility

  • OutcomeV1Schema in @rally/contracts: id, revision, headline, body, status, strategy, refs, source summary (kind, id, title, message count, people), author, acting agent.
  • The Thread turn uses the existing system message type with structuredContent: { kind: "outcome", outcome: OutcomeV1 } and plain fallback text ("Maya merged: <headline>"). A new messageType value would break strict parsers in released desktops; this follows the AutomationDraftProposedV1 card pattern.
  • ChannelFeedItemSchema gains a third kind, outcome, returned only when the client asks with ?kinds=outcome. The thread kind gains an optional latestOutcome summary (headline, status, revision) under the same flag.
  • Rejections name their field through describeZodIssues; a refused merge says which ref was outside the source.

Drafting with a model

  • POST …/outcomes/draft returns a draft and stores nothing. It runs runStructuredInference with a new model role, outcome_draft, and a JSON schema: headline, body, status, decision, reasons, open questions, and turn citations.
  • Input is readableMessagesBefore of the source (what was said, no tool cards), plus the titles and latest versions of its artifacts, plus any child Outcomes. For a Thread, child Outcomes are passed in place of the Breakouts' transcripts, so a Thread summary reads small inputs and never needs the full tree.
  • Citations are validated server side: a cited turn id outside the source drops the claim's citation and marks the line unsupported in the composer.
  • Model: the cheap, fast class already used for Breakout handoff summaries, through the Flagship model flag for the new role. Metered as its own usage activity.
  • Latency: the request starts when the composer opens (or when the menu item is hovered), and the composer shows the non-AI choices immediately, so nobody waits on the model unless they picked Decision record.
  • Consolidate (apply later edits to an earlier long message) is the same endpoint with a different instruction and returns text plus a diff.
  • Wrap-up turn is not a new endpoint: it is a normal prompt to the source's agent, using a skill that knows the Outcome shape and the target's budget.

Agent tool

Extend the rally:conversations plugin with merge_outcome(headline, body?, status, artifacts?, turns?). Scope comes from the loop input in conversationToolAccess: a Breakout loop may merge only to its Thread, a Thread loop only to that Thread's Channels. The author is the person whose turn the run is serving; admission re-checks that person can post in the target. This closes the open question in conversation-tools.md about a Breakout posting to Main without letting a non-member post through the agent.

Reads and staleness

  • Breakout directory rows gain latest_outcome_id and outcome_through_turn_id. "N new since merge" is the source's latest turn compared with that waterline, read from data the chips already carry.
  • "Edited since merge" compares outcome_refs.resource_version with the resource's current_version.
  • Remove: PATCH …/outcomes/:id {state:"removed"}. The projection turn re-renders as "Outcome removed by Maya"; the feed item drops. The resource links stay, since someone may already rely on them; removing them is a separate action.
  • Breakout capture recipe: add outcome turns to the kept types, which is a new recipe revision.

Phasing

  1. Breakout → Thread, no AI. Outcome tables, Pointer, Deliverable and Written, the Thread card under the origin chip, Remove, and the agent tool. Proves the primitive.
  2. Drafting. Decision record, Consolidate, Wrap-up skill, smart default.
  3. Thread → Channel. Feed kind, row state change, Channel waterline, Activity arm, child Outcomes as summary input.
  4. Lifecycle. Revisions in place, "since merge" marks, Merge and archive, Ready to merge suggestion, digests.

Open questions for the team

  • Name. "Merge" fits the founder's model and engineers, but our users are recruiters and operators. Alternatives: "Bring back", "Share result", "Hand back". Recommendation: keep Merge as the verb in menus and call the posted object an Outcome; test with two customers.
  • Channel unread. Does an Outcome move the Channel dot? Recommended yes; it is the Channel's own news, like a placement.
  • Private Thread into a public Channel. Allow it with the explicit exposure line, or require the thread be public first? Recommended allow; merging is the controlled way to share a result without opening the whole thread.
  • Length budgets. Numbers above are placeholders.
  • Who may remove an Outcome. The author and Organization admins? Or anyone who can post in the parent?
  • iOS. Renders the fallback text until it gains the card. Fine for a prototype platform?
