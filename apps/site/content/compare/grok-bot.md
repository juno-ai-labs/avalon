**Short answer:** It depends on who's doing the work. Grok Bot suits someone who wants to build named agents, give each a job, and watch them work a cloud computer. Rally suits a team that wants one AI everyone talks to, in a shared place where each project keeps its own files and history. If you love tinkering with bots, Grok Bot is a treat. If you'd rather the AI organize itself around your team, with a bill you can predict before the month starts, go with Rally.

## Where Rally wins

### A price you can plan around

Rally costs $100 or $250 a month for the whole organization. Add a teammate and the bill stays the same.

### Nobody has to own the bot

Describe a job in plain words, like a weekly summary or a follow-up after every sales call, and Rally builds the Sidekick for it. There are no system prompts to write and no skills files to maintain, and everyone it is shared with talks to it in one conversation, so nobody ends up as the team's bot keeper.

### Every piece of work gets its own room

A Canvas holds one piece of work: the conversation, the files, and every version of what Rally made. It stays private until you share it, and context stays where the work happened, so a new question starts from what the team already knows.

### Everyone's access stays their own

Everyone in a Canvas talks to the same Rally, and it tracks who asked what and who is allowed to see what, across people and connectors. A shared AI can help the whole team without spilling one person's access into another's view.

> Rally is very generous with new projects. Every one of them gets a blank Canvas.
