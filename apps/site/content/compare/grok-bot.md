**Short answer:** It depends on who's doing the work. Grok Bot suits someone who wants to build named agents, give each a job, and watch them work a cloud computer. Rally suits a team that wants one AI everyone talks to, in a shared place where each project keeps its own files and history. If you love tinkering with bots, Grok Bot is a treat. If you'd rather the AI organize itself around your team, with a bill you can predict before the month starts, go with Rally.

## Side by side

| | Rally | Grok Bot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built for | Teams who work with one shared AI | People who build and run their own named agents |
| People in one AI conversation | Yes. Everyone in a Channel talks to the same Rally. | Team Bots (public beta) can join Slack channels; in the app, each teammate has a private chat |
| Shared context | Spaces and Channels keep files, decisions, and history for people and AI alike. | A Team Bot has shared team memory plus private notes per person |
| Private side work | Breakouts for one-on-one or small-group work, merged back when ready. | Each teammate's direct chat with a Team Bot stays private |
| Your tools | Google Workspace, Slack, Notion, GitHub, Linear, Granola, and more. Reads and writes back. | A plugin catalog, plus computer use on a cloud machine for everything else |
| Runs on its own | Sidekicks run on a schedule or when something happens. | Bots save skills and rerun them on a schedule, on a cloud computer |
| Models | One voice, with each job routed to the model family best at it. | Grok models from xAI |
| Pricing | $100 or $250 per organization a month. No seat limit. | Included in Cursor plans (Teams is $40 per user a month) or a linked SuperGrok plan; weekly allowance, then on-demand |

## Where Rally wins

### A price you can plan around

Rally costs $100 or $250 a month for the whole organization. Add a teammate and the bill stays the same.

### Nobody has to own the bot

Rally creates its own Sidekicks as tasks need them, like a weekly digest or a follow-up after every sales call. There are no system prompts to write and no skills files to maintain, so nobody ends up as the team's bot keeper.

### Every project gets its own room

Spaces hold a project, Channels hold the shared conversations inside it, and Breakouts hold the private side work until it is ready to merge back. Context stays where the work happened, so a new question starts from what the team already knows.

### Everyone's access stays their own

Everyone in a Channel talks to the same Rally, and it tracks who asked what and who is allowed to see what, across people and connectors. A shared AI can help the whole team without spilling one person's access into another's view.
