§ 1

The docket

Your inbox is the matter list, written down.

Each request the business emails you becomes a numbered line. Look at two things first: whose turn it is, and for how many days. Nobody enters a matter, and nobody updates a stage. When an email arrives, Rally changes the line.

§ 2

Whose turn it is

Every email passes the turn.

A matter is always in one of three places: with legal, with the business, or with the other side. An email moves it.

§ 3

The NDA queue

NDAs: in, out, back.

NDA requests go to whoever the business thinks of first. So build the queue from several inboxes. Each person on the legal team connects their own Gmail and allows it. From then on their mail feeds one queue, and each line shows whose inbox it came through.

Rally marks the request coming in, your standard paper going out and the signed copy coming back. Turnaround is counted in days. Nobody logs a request, and nobody ticks a box.

If the other side asks for changes instead of signing, the line says so. A lawyer takes it from there.

One inbox
A solo general counsel connects one Gmail. That is enough.
Several
Each teammate connects their own. Nobody’s mail is read until they do.

§ 4

A status page for the business

Let the people who ask look it up.

Share a status page with the business. They open it and see where their request stands, in plain words. When the honest answer is “waiting on Sales”, the page says so.

Share it with one teammate, with a guest by email, or with the whole company. Each page has its own access. Sharing the status page shares that page and nothing else.

It is built from the same mail as the docket, and it updates itself the same way. Nobody on either side keeps it up.

§ 5

For the business, nothing changes

No intake form to roll out.

The business keeps emailing you, as it does now. There is no form to fill in, no portal to log in to and nothing for them to learn.

Nothing changes for you either. You do not tag, label or log anything. Rally sits on top of the Gmail you already use and adds one window. It reads each request as it arrives and updates the pages on its own.

§ 6

How to start

Three steps. No project.

  1. 1

    Connect Gmail

    Sign up for free and connect your Gmail. Google asks you to approve it once. There is nothing to install.

  2. 2

    Say what you want to see

    Type one sentence. Rally reads the mail and builds the page. For example:

    • “List every open legal request in my inbox: who asked, the stage, whose turn it is and for how many days.”
    • “Show the same matters in three lanes: with legal, with the business, with the other side.”
    • “Make a queue of NDA requests from my inbox, Theo’s and Priya’s, with turnaround in days.”
    • “Make a status page I can share with the business.”
  3. 3

    It keeps itself current

    Each time an email arrives, Rally updates the page. You never refresh it and you never fill it in. If a matter is in the wrong place, say so in a sentence and Rally changes it.

§ 7

Your mail

What Rally can do with your mail, and what it cannot.

You are connecting your inbox. You should know exactly what that allows before you do it.

Rally can

  • Search and read your mail. A docket needs nothing more.
  • Write drafts. They wait in your Gmail Drafts.
  • Send mail, and label, archive or move mail to Trash, only when you ask it to.

Rally cannot

  • Delete mail for good. It has no way to.
  • Read an inbox its owner has not connected.

You decide

  • Rally reads mail only after you connect Gmail yourself.
  • Each teammate connects their own inbox. Nobody’s mail is read until they do.
  • You can remove the connection at any time.
