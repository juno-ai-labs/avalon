Who to chase

Who still owes you what.

Half the job is chasing. Rally reads what you asked for and what came back, and keeps the list: who has gone quiet, what is still missing, and how many times you have asked.

You never update it. A client replies, and the list changes on its own. If they send half, the half that arrived is crossed off and the rest stays on the list.

Rally writes the reminder. You send it.

Ask, and Rally drafts each reminder. It thanks the client for what they sent and asks only for what is missing. The draft waits in your Gmail Drafts. It goes nowhere until you read it and press send.

What is due

Due dates on their days, not in someone’s head.

Tell Rally which dates you track, or point it at the Google Calendar you already keep. Each filing sits on its day. When the confirmation lands in your inbox, Rally flips it from due to filed. Nobody marks it by hand.

Rally does not know tax law and does not work out a due date for you. It shows the dates you give it, and what your mail says about each one.

The whole practice

One checklist from every preparer’s inbox.

A page can be built from one inbox or from several. Each preparer connects their own Gmail and allows it. From then on, their client mail feeds the same checklist, and every row shows whose client it is.

Nobody’s inbox is read until they connect it themselves. Share the page with the whole practice or with one teammate: each page has its own access. Rally is priced per team, not per seat.

Busy season

Nothing new to keep up in the weeks you have no time.

The week before a deadline is the wrong week to update a tracker. With Rally there is no tracker to update. The page updates itself each time mail arrives, in March as in July.

Clients change nothing

They reply to your email with the attachment, days late, as they always have. Nobody learns a portal. Nobody gets a login.

You change nothing

Keep asking by email. Nobody logs a request, tags a thread or ticks a box. Rally reads the mail where it is.

It sits on top of what you have

Keep your tax software, your ledger and your portal, if you have one. Rally adds one page, built from the mail you already send and get.

How to start

Three steps, between two client emails.

  1. 01

    Connect Gmail

    Sign up for free and connect the Gmail you use with clients. Google asks you to approve it once. It takes about five minutes, start to finish.

  2. 02

    Say what you want to see

    Type one sentence, the way you would say it to a colleague.

    • “Make a checklist of the documents each client owes me for the October close.”
    • “Who am I waiting on, and how many times have I asked?”
    • “Put my filing dates on a calendar and mark each one filed when the confirmation arrives.”
  3. 03

    It keeps itself up to date

    Each time a client emails, Rally updates the page on its own. You never fill it in or refresh it. Want payroll split out, or a column for 1099s? Say so, and Rally changes it.

Your clients’ mail

What Rally can and cannot do with it.

Client financial mail is sensitive, so here it is plainly. Rally reads your mail only after you connect Gmail yourself, and you can remove the connection at any time. A teammate’s inbox is read only after they connect it themselves.

Rally has completed an independent security assessment. Whether that meets your firm’s own rules for client data is your call, so the controls are written down for you to read.

See our security controls

Rally can

  • Search and read the mail in the Gmail you connect. Pages like these only need to read.
  • Write a draft, when you ask. It waits in Gmail Drafts until you send it.
  • Send an email, or label, archive, mark as spam or move mail to Trash. Only when you ask.

Rally does not

  • Change anything in your mailbox unless you ask it to.
  • Delete mail for good. It has no way to.
  • Read inside a PDF or a statement. It goes by what the email says and the names of the files attached.

So the page tells you what has arrived, not whether it is right. A file named as a bank statement is ticked as one. Whether it is the right month is still yours to check.
