Every manual has a chapter like this one, near the back, where the tone changes
from "here is what you can do" to "here is what to do when it does not". Most
readers never open it. If you are reading it now, something has probably gone
a little sideways, and we are sorry. Let us sort it out.

Rally rarely fails quietly. When it cannot do something,
it says so in a sentence, and that sentence is usually specific: a link it will
not follow, a connection that needs you to sign in again, a run that stopped
with a reason and a **Retry** button. Most of this chapter is a guide to those
sentences. Find the one on your screen, and the fix is next to it.

Two things are worth knowing first.

First, Rally does not retry on its own when it matters. If a link fails, it
will not quietly try it again, because sign-in and connection links are often
good for one use only. If a run changed something in another tool and then
broke, Rally will not repeat that change behind your back, because sending the
same email twice is worse than sending it once. So after you fix the cause, you
take the next step yourself: click the link again, press **Retry**, or start
**Connect** again. That is a little more work for you and a lot less surprise.

Second, most problems turn out to be about which account you are using. You
are signed in with a different account from the one an invitation was sent to. A connection
belongs to the Organization, not to you, so only an administrator can refresh
it. A Canvas was never shared with you. When something will not open,
ask yourself which account you are using and who owns the thing you are
reaching for. That usually leads to an owner or administrator in your
Organization, who can fix it faster than anyone else.

When you have tried the obvious and Rally is still being stubborn, a person at
Rally will help. This chapter says how to reach one and what to send so the
first reply is useful. It also lists what never to put in an email, however
helpful it seems at the time.

It ends with the glossary: every Rally word in one alphabetical list. That
includes the old names one last time. An Organization used to be a Workspace,
and a Sidekick was an Automation. Before Canvases, Rally kept its conversations
in Channels, Threads and Breakouts, which were removed when Rally moved to
Canvases and Sidekicks. The app has mostly caught up, but here and
there a button or a message still uses the old word. If you spot one, it is not
a different feature, only an older label.
