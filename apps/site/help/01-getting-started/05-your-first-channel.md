Time to give Rally a job. A Channel is one conversation about one piece of work, so the first thing to decide is what that work is. It can be small. "Plan the team offsite" is a Channel. So is "Why is the invoice from March wrong?"

## Start a Channel

1. Click the **+** button (**New channel or space**) at the top of the Spaces rail.
2. Choose **Start New Channel**.
3. In the **Start a new channel** dialog, pick a **Space** from the list. In a new Organization the only choice is **General**, which makes this the easiest decision you will make all day.
4. Type a **Name of channel**, up to 180 characters. A few words is plenty.
5. Click **Create**.

You can also right-click an empty part of the Channels rail. That menu offers the same Channel choices, already set to the Space you are looking at.

The **+** menu has two other items. **Create New Space** makes a new group for Channels, covered in [Spaces](../03-spaces-and-breakouts/01-spaces.md). **Import Channel from Claude or ChatGPT** brings in a conversation you had elsewhere, covered in [Bring in a conversation from Claude or ChatGPT](../02-working-in-a-channel/08-import-from-claude-or-chatgpt.md).

## Write the opening message

Clicking **Create** does not make the Channel yet. It opens a starting screen with your Channel's name at the top, and the Channel comes into being the moment you send its first message. Until then, nothing has been saved, and you can close the screen without leaving an empty Channel lying around.

The starting screen has two parts.

- **Participants** at the top, for bringing teammates in from the start. Click **Add people** and type a name. Matches are numbered, so <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>9</kbd> picks one without reaching for the mouse. If the Space is private, this bar says **Managed by the space** instead, because the Space decides who is in its Channels. On your own in a brand new Organization, you can leave it alone.
- A message box that says **Describe what this channel will be used for**. Type what you want Rally to do. Press <kbd>Return</kbd> to create the Channel and send, or <kbd>⇧ Shift</kbd> <kbd>Return</kbd> for a new line.

Mentioning a teammate by name in that first message adds them to the Channel too. [People in the room](../02-working-in-a-channel/05-people-in-the-room.md) has more on who is in a Channel.

The new Channel appears in the Channels rail as soon as you press <kbd>Return</kbd>, and Rally starts on your message straight away.

### What to ask

Write to Rally the way you would write to a capable colleague: say what you want and what it is for. If you are stuck for a first request, try something like this:

> Find out what the three most popular project management apps charge for a team of ten, and write me a one-page comparison as a document.

That sends Rally to the web, has it read and compare, and ends with a document you can open, which shows off a good deal of what it does in one go. None of it needs a connection to your other tools. Once you have connected some, try asking about your own calendar, issues or documents instead.

## Watch Rally work

While Rally works, a row with a small animated orb shows what it is doing and for how long:

- **Thinking for 4s** while it works out its approach.
- **Using Slack for 8s**, or the name of whichever tool it is using, with that tool's logo.
- **Working for 1m 12s** on longer stretches.

When each step finishes, the row switches to the past tense: **Thought for 4s**, **Used Slack for 12s**. Once the answer is in, all of that folds into one line, such as **Worked for 2m 05s**. Click the line, or its arrow, to open it and see each step Rally took.

The answer itself appears as Rally writes it, a few words at a time, with a small pulsing caret at the end. You can start reading straight away. The conversation follows the newest text as it arrives. If you scroll up to read something earlier, it stops following and a round **Jump to latest** arrow appears above the message box to take you back down.

> [!NOTE]
> If Rally's AI provider is having a slow moment, a notice says **The model is currently slowed down. Responses might take longer.** Nothing is wrong on your side; the answer is still coming.

Some jobs take a few seconds and some take much longer. You do not have to watch. Go and do something else, and the Channel's status dot and badge will tell you when it is done or needs you. [While Rally works](../02-working-in-a-channel/03-while-rally-works.md) covers following along, steering, stopping and what to do if a run fails.

## Names and icons

The Channel you just started keeps the name you gave it. Rally does not rename a Channel that someone has named.

Some conversations start without a name, though: a Breakout you branch off from a message, a Channel started from Slack, or a conversation you import. For those, Rally names them itself.

- At first, a shimmering grey bar sits where the name will go. That is Rally reading the opening request.
- A few seconds later, a short name of two to four words replaces it, in the language of your request, and Rally picks an icon to suit the subject.
- After the conversation has had more than five messages, Rally takes one more look and may improve the name, now that the real subject has had time to show up. It does this once, and then the name stays put.
- If no name arrives within about a minute, Rally gives up waiting and uses the first line of the opening message as the name.

Renaming a conversation yourself settles the matter: Rally never overwrites a name a person chose. The same goes for an icon you pick yourself.

Every Channel gets an icon and colour, even if nobody chooses one. To change them, click the icon at the left of the Channel's header (**Change channel icon and color**), pick a colour, then an icon. [Names, icons and colours](../02-working-in-a-channel/07-name-icon-and-colour.md) covers renaming and everything else about how a Channel looks. A Channel with a nice icon is not more productive, but it is easier to find, which is nearly the same thing.

## An empty Channel

If you open a Channel that has no messages in it yet, it says **Ready to begin** and **Send a message to start work in this stream.** That is the old word "stream" again, meaning this Channel. Type in the box at the bottom and Rally gets going.
