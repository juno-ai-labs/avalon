In the last chapter you made your first Channel and asked Rally for something.
This chapter is about what happens after that, which is most of the time you will
spend in Rally.

## One room, one piece of work

A Channel is one conversation about one piece of work. Not a topic, not a
department, not a place where announcements go to be ignored. "Plan the March
offsite" is a good Channel. "Marketing" is a filing cabinet.

Inside that conversation everyone talks in the same room. You write, your
teammates write, and Rally, the agent, writes too. Nobody has to summon Rally
from a corner with a special command, and nobody has to copy its answer back
into the "real" conversation afterwards. Most chat apps treat their bot like the
office printer: you walk over to it, you collect what it made, you carry it back
to your desk. In Rally the agent sits at the table with everyone else. It reads
what you all write, picks up the parts meant for it, and leaves the rest of the
chat to the humans.

That has a pleasant side effect. Everything about the piece of work collects in
one place. The questions, the decisions, the files you dropped in, the drafts
Rally wrote and the people who weighed in are all in the same scroll, in the
order they happened. When someone new joins next week, you add them to the
Channel and they can read the whole story instead of asking you to "catch them
up" over a coffee you did not want to buy.

## Two ideas that make the rest click

**Rally does real work, and work takes time.** When you ask for something, Rally
starts a *run*: a stretch of work where it reads, searches, writes and uses your
connected tools until it has an answer. A run might take four seconds or forty
minutes. You do not have to sit and watch. You can keep talking, change your
mind halfway, or stop it, and the Channel shows you what it is up to the whole
time.

**The Channel tells you when it needs you.** Small coloured dots and number
badges say whether Rally is busy, waiting on an answer from you, finished, or
stuck. Once you know the colours, you can glance at your list of Channels and
know where to look first, the way you glance at a kettle and know whether to
reach for a mug.

## What this chapter covers

The pages that follow walk through a Channel from the bottom of the window to the
top. You start with the message box, where you type, mention people, dictate
and use a few keyboard tricks. Then you hand Rally files to read. Next comes
the part people ask about most: what is going on while Rally works, how to steer
it, and what to do when a run fails. After that you learn to get the most out of
Rally's answers, with their tables, sources, diagrams and the small actions
under each message.

Then the people. You will see how to bring teammates into a Channel, how to tell
who is around, and when Rally decides to speak up or stay quiet. You will learn
the colours of the status dots, and how to give a Channel a proper name, an icon
and a colour so you can find it again. The chapter ends with a way to bring a
conversation you started in Claude or ChatGPT into Rally, so that work does not
have to start from scratch.

By the end you will know your way around a Channel well enough to stop thinking
about the app and think about the work instead, which is the whole point.
