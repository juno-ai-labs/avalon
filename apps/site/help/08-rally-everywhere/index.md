---
title: Rally outside the app
description: Use Rally with Slack and email, choose how it tells you what needs you, and use it in a web browser.
---

So far this book has kept you at your desk, in the Mac app, with a Canvas or a
Sidekick open in front of you. That is where Rally does its best work, and
where you set everything up. But work has a habit of happening somewhere else.
A question turns up in Slack. A customer emails. Someone mentions you while you
are on a train, or a client wants to see one Canvas and has no intention of
installing anything.

This chapter is about those places.

Slack and email are where your Sidekicks meet the rest of your work. A Sidekick
can watch Slack channels and start a run when something is said there, take
work by email at its own address, and send its results back to Slack as a
message your team can reply to. A reply in the Slack thread under a Sidekick's
message carries the same run on, right where it spoke. What Slack and email do
not do any more is start a conversation of their own: mentioning Rally in Slack
or emailing Rally does not create anything in Rally. The way to hand Rally work
from Slack or email is a Sidekick with a trigger.

Rally can also reach you. When someone mentions you in a Canvas or a Sidekick's
conversation, when a Sidekick needs input from you, or when a run you care
about finishes, Rally tells you, on your Mac, in your browser or in Slack.

And Rally runs in a web browser, for the client who should see one Canvas, the
teammate on a borrowed computer, and you on your phone. It shows the same
Canvases and Sidekicks your team sees on their Macs. There is one Rally, and
you pick the door.

> [!NOTE]
> Slack calls its own top-level thing a **workspace**. A Slack workspace is not
> your Rally Organization, even though Rally itself used to call Organizations
> "workspaces" and you may still see the old word here and there. In this
> chapter, "Slack workspace" always means Slack's, and "Organization" always
> means Rally's.

The chapter starts with Slack, because that is where most teams already talk.
Then come notifications, and it ends with the browser.
