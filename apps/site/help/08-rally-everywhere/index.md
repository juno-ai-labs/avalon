---
title: Rally outside the app
description: Use Rally from Slack, email, your iPhone and a web browser, and choose how it tells you what needs you.
---

So far this book has kept you at your desk, in the Mac app, with a Channel open
in front of you. That is where Rally does its best work, and where you set
everything up. But work has a habit of happening somewhere else. A question
turns up in Slack. A customer replies by email. Someone mentions you while you
are on a train, or a client wants to see one conversation and has no intention
of installing anything.

This chapter is about all of those places. Rally reaches out in two directions.

**You can reach Rally from elsewhere.** You can hand it work from Slack with a
direct message, an **@Rally** mention, the `/rally` command or a shortcut, and
get the finished answer back in the same Slack thread. You can forward an email
into any Channel, because every Channel has its own email address. You can read
and reply from your iPhone. And people you share a conversation with can join
from an ordinary web browser.

**Rally can reach you.** When someone mentions you or adds you to a Channel,
Rally tells you, on your Mac, on your iPhone or in Slack. And if you would
rather not be told about everything the moment it happens, Rally can write you
a digest instead: a short summary of what moved, delivered on the days and at
the times you pick.

One idea makes the rest of the chapter click: **these are all doors into the
same rooms.** A thread you start in Slack becomes a Channel in Rally. A reply
you type on your iPhone lands in the same conversation your teammates see on
their Macs. An email you forward shows up in the Channel it was addressed to.
Nothing is copied into a separate Slack Rally or phone Rally. There is one
Rally, and you are choosing which door to walk through.

The second idea is a matter of proportion. The Mac app is the workshop. The
other doors are for following along and joining in. Creating Spaces and
Sidekicks, connecting tools and changing most settings still happen on the Mac,
and the pages that follow say plainly what each door can and cannot do, so you
do not spend an afternoon hunting for a button that lives somewhere else.

> [!NOTE]
> Slack calls its own top-level thing a **workspace**. A Slack workspace is not
> your Rally Organization, even though Rally itself used to call Organizations
> "workspaces" and you may still see the old word here and there. In this
> chapter, "Slack workspace" always means Slack's, and "Organization" always
> means Rally's.

The chapter starts with Slack, because that is where most teams already talk,
then moves to email, notifications and the digest, and ends with the iPhone
app and the browser. Think of it as Rally's travel guide: it packs light, and
it always knows the way home.
