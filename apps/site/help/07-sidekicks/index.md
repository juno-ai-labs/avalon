---
title: Sidekicks
description: Save a helper once and let it run on a schedule, when something happens, or whenever you call on it.
---

Everything in this book so far has had one thing in common: you were there.
You typed a message, Rally got to work, and you watched it happen. That is a
fine arrangement for most jobs. It is a poor one for the Monday report, the
morning inbox sweep, or the support channel that needs watching while you are
asleep. Rally is willing to work nights. Somebody has to tell it what to do
when nobody is looking.

That is what a Sidekick is for. A Sidekick is a named helper that you describe
once, in plain words, and then stop describing. It has a name, a short
description, a set of written instructions and, if you want it to act on its
own, one or more triggers that say when to start. You never fill in a form to
make one. You talk to it, the same way you talk to Rally in a Channel, and it
writes its own instructions down.

## Two kinds of helper

The one idea that makes this chapter click is that Sidekicks come in two
flavours, and the difference is whether they have a trigger.

- **Instructions only.** A Sidekick with no trigger never starts anything by
  itself. It is a page of know-how that Rally can pick up in any conversation
  where it is relevant: your brand voice, the way your team writes release
  notes, the checklist for a new client. Write it once and every conversation
  can borrow it.
- **Delegated work.** A Sidekick with a trigger runs on its own. Every Monday
  at 9, every hour, whenever a new meeting note lands, whenever someone emails
  it. Each time, it does its job and reports back where you told it to.

The second idea is ownership. A Sidekick belongs to you alone, to the whole
Organization, or to one Channel, and that decides who can see it, who can
change it, and whose connected tools it uses when it runs.

## A word about the old name

Sidekicks used to be called Automations. The app has nearly finished the
move, but a few corners still use the old word: Rally's Home tab in Slack
talks about creating an automation, some descriptions in **Settings** say
"automations" where they mean Sidekicks, and some link addresses still
contain `automations`. They are all the same thing. Nobody has been
replaced; the Sidekick got a better name tag.

## Where this chapter goes

The pages ahead start with what a Sidekick is and who it belongs to, then
show you how to make one by describing it. After that come the triggers that
start a Sidekick, the window where you manage them all, where a Sidekick's
results end up, and finally how to bring a Sidekick into a Channel so the
whole room can use it. By the end, Rally will be doing a fair share of your
week without being asked twice.
