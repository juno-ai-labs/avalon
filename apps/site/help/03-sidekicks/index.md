Everything in the last chapter had one thing in common: you were there. You
typed a message, Rally got to work, and you watched it happen. That is a fine
arrangement for most jobs and a poor one for the Monday report, the morning
inbox sweep, or the support queue that needs watching while you are asleep.
Rally is willing to work nights. Somebody has to tell it what to do when nobody
is looking.

That is what a Sidekick is: an AI agent with one job, which your team shares
like a colleague. You describe the job once, in plain words, and then stop
describing it. The Sidekick writes its own instructions down, decides with you
when it should wake up, and does the work each time, reporting back in its
conversation and wherever else you asked.

## One agent, one conversation

Every Sidekick has a single conversation, and everyone it is shared with sees
the same one. People talk to each other there, and when someone addresses the
Sidekick ("can you rerun last week's numbers?"), it starts a run. Each run
shows in the conversation as a folded row that carries its reply, and anything
it made for people to work on afterwards shows as a card that opens a
[Canvas](../02-canvases/index.md).

That one conversation is also where the Sidekick is set up and changed. A
message addressed to the Sidekick starts a run, whoever sends it. The people
who manage a Sidekick change it by marking a message **Edit Sidekick** ("make
it run at 8 instead"), which goes to its drafting assistant and saves a new
version of the Sidekick. Runs
and setup stay separate underneath, so you can always see which run did what,
under which version.

## Who it works for

A Sidekick is shared with just you, with particular people, or with the whole
Organization, and that decides who can talk to it. Separately, it keeps a list
of the people it may act as when it uses their connected tools. Joining the
conversation does not put you on that list. The two lists are different on
purpose, and the chapter explains both, because a helper that can read your
Gmail deserves a minute's thought about who else can ask it to.
