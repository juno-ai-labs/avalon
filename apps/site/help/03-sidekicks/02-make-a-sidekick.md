There is no Sidekick form, and nowhere to tick "include weekends". You make a
Sidekick the way you ask Rally for anything: say what you want, and a drafting
assistant works out the details and writes them down.

## Start a new Sidekick

Click **Sidekicks** in the rail, then **New Sidekick** on the Sidekicks home
page.

Rally shows **Creating Sidekick…** for a moment, then opens your new
Sidekick. In the middle is its conversation, and beside it the details column.
While the Sidekick is a draft, the conversation is with the drafting assistant,
and nobody else can see it.

If something goes wrong you see **Sidekick could not be created** with a
**Try again** button.

## Describe what you want

Write to the drafting assistant as you would to a capable colleague. Say
what the job is, when it should happen, and where the result should go:

> Every Friday at 4pm, look through the #support channel in Slack for
> questions we did not answer, and make me a list with links.

The assistant asks follow-up questions when it needs to: which Slack
channel, what counts as "unanswered", whether you want an empty list on quiet
weeks. Answer them in plain words. When it has what it needs, it saves the
Sidekick straight away and tells you so in the conversation. There is no
**Create** or **Save** button to look for; saving is the assistant's job.

Each save fills in or updates:

- the **name** and **description**;
- the **instructions** the Sidekick follows;
- any **triggers** (see [Triggers and schedules](04-triggers-and-schedules.md));
- an **icon** and **colour**, picked to suit the job.

Until the assistant has enough to save a working version, the Sidekick stays
a **Draft**. A half-finished draft never replaces the last version that
worked.

## Keep talking to change it

The conversation does not end when the Sidekick is saved. It becomes the
Sidekick's shared conversation, and the people who manage the Sidekick can
come back any time and ask for changes: a new time, a shorter summary, a
different name, a different icon. The details column suggests some under
**Make it work for you**: "Keep it short" or "Use a more casual tone."

Once the Sidekick is saved, a message you send in its conversation asks it
to do its job and starts a run. To change the Sidekick instead, choose
**Edit Sidekick** in the message box before you send, and the message goes to
the drafting assistant. Rally does not guess which you meant; the mark decides.
Once the Sidekick is shared, other people in the conversation see these
changes being made. [Talking to a Sidekick](03-the-shared-conversation.md#managers-change-the-sidekick-here-too)
has the details.

Every change is saved as a new version. A run that has already started
finishes with the version it started with, so editing a Sidekick halfway
through a run will not confuse it mid-sentence.

New Sidekicks start private to you. To share one, tell the drafting
assistant who with ("share it with the support team", "make it an
Organization Sidekick"), as an **Edit Sidekick** message once the Sidekick is
saved, or use **Share** in the details column.

## Change the icon and colour yourself

The drafting assistant chooses an icon and colour, but the hexagon is yours
to repaint. Click the hexagon in the Sidekick's details column to open the
palette, then pick from the **Color** ring and the **Icon** grid. Each click
saves at once, and the palette stays open while you try a few. Every window
showing that Sidekick updates to match.

A recognisable hexagon is worth a minute: it is how you spot the Sidekick in
the sidebar and in its conversation. Changing it
needs permission to manage the Sidekick; if you lack it, the palette shows an
error and the hexagon goes back to how it was.

## Or ask Rally in a Canvas

You do not have to leave a Canvas to make a Sidekick. Ask Rally there:

> Every Friday, update this Canvas with the week's new support tickets.

Rally makes a new Sidekick for you and passes your request to its drafting
assistant. You see the work in the conversation under a **Sidekicks** row, and
the new Sidekick appears in your Sidekicks list, shared with only you until you
share it. A Sidekick made this way knows which Canvas you asked from, so it can
write to that Canvas.

## Writing good instructions

The drafting assistant turns what you say into instructions, so a vague
request gets you a vague Sidekick. These habits help.

- **Say where the result goes.** "Update this Canvas", "send me a Slack
  DM", "email the summary to me". Beyond its own conversation, a Sidekick
  delivers only where you tell it to; see
  [Where a Sidekick's results go](07-where-results-go.md).
- **Say what to do when there is nothing to report.** "If nothing new came
  in, say nothing" keeps quiet weeks quiet.
- **Name the sources.** "The #support channel", "my Gmail inbox", "new
  Granola notes". Rally can only use tools that are connected; see
  [Connecting your other tools](../05-connecting-your-tools/index.md).
- **Describe the shape of a good answer.** "Three bullet points, newest
  first, with links" beats "a summary".
- **Name the time zone** if it matters: "9am Pacific".

> [!NOTE]
> A Sidekick's name can be up to 140 characters, its description up to 1,200
> and its instructions up to 12,000. The assistant keeps within these, but if
> you paste in a whole handbook it will need trimming.
